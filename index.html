Real-World Awareness

What Is Gender-Based Violence?

Violence is not always physical or immediately visible. It takes root across everyday spaces. Select an environment below to explore how violence manifests and the warning signs to recognize.

Domestic & Intimate Spheres

"It often starts with control, isolation, and jealousy before escalating into physical danger."

Home should be a sanctuary, but domestic violence thrives behind closed doors where secrecy protects the abuser. Abuse is rarely an isolated conflict — it is a sustained cycle of coercive control.

Spousal & Family Terror

Domestic Violence

Coercive, violent, or threatening behaviour committed by an intimate partner, ex-partner, or family member in the home. It often cycles between escalating tension, intimidation, and false remorse to keep a person trapped.

Bodily Force

Physical Violence

Direct bodily harm such as slapping, hitting, choking, physical restraint, confinement, or attacks using household objects.

Mental Control

Emotional & Psychological Abuse

Sustained verbal belittling, gaslighting, threats of harm, and deliberately isolating someone from family and supportive friendships.

Public & Professional Spaces

"Where authority and unequal power are abused to silence complaints."

Spaces meant for education, professional advancement, and public life become hostile when physical boundaries are crossed and complaints are dismissed.

Work & Public Safety

Sexual Harassment

Unwelcome sexual advances, vulgar remarks, intrusive staring, inappropriate touching, or implicit demands where career progression or academic grades are conditioned on sexual favours.

Non-Consensual Violations

Sexual Violence

Any sexual act or attempted assault forced upon an individual without their freely given consent, through intimidation, physical force, or intoxication, including marital rape.

Digital Networks & Devices

"Technology gives abusers constant, invasive reach into personal life."

Smartphones and online platforms enable perpetrators to stalk, monitor, and humiliate survivors across physical boundaries without ever entering the room.

Online & Cyber Violence

Online & Digital Violence

The weaponisation of smartphones, messaging apps, and social platforms to monitor, terrorise, or publicly humiliate someone. This includes installing hidden GPS stalkerware, relentless online threats, doxxing personal addresses, and distributing private intimate photos without consent.

Economic & Cultural Pressures

"Stripping away financial means and autonomy so leaving feels impossible."

Abuse is often sustained through enforced financial dependence and customary pressure, making external support and legal knowledge crucial for escape.

Financial Autonomy

Economic Abuse

Taking away bank cards, strictly withholding money for food and medical care, or forbidding someone from working to maintain total control and dependency.

Stolen Autonomy

Child or Forced Marriage

Pressuring or forcing an underage minor into marriage due to debts or customary norms, cutting short their schooling, health, and personal autonomy.

A Note on Protection for Everyone

While women and girls face the highest rates of recorded gender-based violence in Malaysia, violence impacts people of every gender, age, and background. Every person who experiences abuse deserves legal protection, dignified care, and immediate support without judgment.

Statutory Frameworks

Law Enforcement & Legal Protection

Malaysian law provides enforceable statutory frameworks designed to penalize perpetrators, criminalize intimidation, and grant immediate civil protection orders for survivors.

Act 521

Domestic Violence Act 1994

Provides civil remedies and immediate protection orders for victims abused by a current spouse, former spouse, or domestic family member residing in the household.

  • Emergency Protection Order (EPO): Issued directly within 2 hours by Social Welfare (JKM) officers, lasting 7 days without prior court hearings.
  • Interim Protection Order (IPO): Granted by a Magistrate Court during police investigation, barring the abuser from contact.
  • Protection Order (PO): Granted after trial or settlement, granting exclusive occupation of the residence to the survivor.
Act 574

Penal Code Provisions

Defines criminal investigation, police arrest authority, and prison sentences for physical assault, sexual violence, and stalking offenses.

  • Section 354: Assault or use of criminal force with intent to outrage modesty (imprisonment up to 10 years, fine, or whipping).
  • Section 375 & 376: Rape and statutory rape (punishable with 10 to 20 years rigorous imprisonment and mandatory whipping).
  • Section 507A (Anti-Stalking): Criminalizes repeated following, tracking with GPS, or communicating that causes distress or fear.
Act 840

Anti-Sexual Harassment Act 2022

Establishes a specialized statutory Tribunal for Anti-Sexual Harassment to adjudicate sexual harassment claims quickly, affordably, and confidentially.

  • Tribunal Compensation: Can award civil compensation up to RM250,000 for damages suffered.
  • Mandatory Remedies: Compels perpetrators to issue formal written apologies and attend rehabilitation seminars.
  • Universal Jurisdiction: Covers harassment occurring in workplaces, universities, public venues, and digital platforms.

Institutional Authorities

Enforcers & Authorized Agencies

These are the official governmental bodies and statutory institutions in Malaysia empowered by law to investigate crimes, execute protection orders, and provide crisis intervention.

Criminal Law & Police Investigation Federal Enforcement

Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) • D11 Division

The Sexual, Women and Child Investigation Division (D11) operates specialized investigation units across all district police headquarters (IPD). They are legally mandated to execute arrests, investigate violence, gather forensic evidence, and support survivors in obtaining court IPO orders.

Authorized Powers: Emergency patrol dispatch (999), arrest execution, forensic evidence collection, and hospital police statement intake.
Welfare & Protective Orders Ministry of Women (KPWKM)

Social Welfare Department (JKM)

Under Act 521, authorized Social Welfare Officers (Pegawai Kebajikan Masyarakat) hold the statutory authority to issue Emergency Protection Orders (EPOs) on the spot without waiting for a Magistrate court hearing.

Authorized Powers: Issuing 7-day EPO orders within 2 hours, government safe house placement, emergency financial aid, and child welfare custody.
Clinical Forensic & Medical Triage Ministry of Health (KKM)

One Stop Crisis Centre (OSCC)

Operating 24/7 in the Emergency & Trauma Departments of all Malaysian general hospitals, the OSCC brings medical officers, police officers, and welfare officers under one roof so survivors do not have to recount their trauma at multiple locations.

Authorized Powers: Forensic injury documentation, clinical rape kit examination, PEP medication within 72 hours, and on-site police statement lodging.
Civil Redress & Adjudication Statutory Tribunal

Tribunal for Anti-Sexual Harassment (TAGS)

Established following the gazetting of Act 840, the Tribunal functions as a fast-track statutory judicial body to hear sexual harassment complaints within 60 days, providing accessible civil compensation and accountability without court fees.

Authorized Powers: Legally binding awards up to RM250,000, issuing injunctions, orders for publication of formal apologies, and criminal penalties for non-compliance.

One Stop Crisis Centre (OSCC) Protocol

Every major government hospital in Malaysia provides an OSCC inside its Emergency & Trauma Department:

  1. Step 1: Approach the Emergency triage counter and state that you need the OSCC.
  2. Step 2: A doctor performs a confidential medical examination and documents injuries.
  3. Step 3: If within 72 hours, PEP medication and emergency contraception are administered.
  4. Step 4: A Social Welfare (JKM) officer issues an Emergency Protection Order (EPO).
  5. Step 5: Police take your statement directly in the hospital without requiring you to visit a police station.
